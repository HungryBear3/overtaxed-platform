## Why the Reassessment Year Matters

Cook County reassesses on a three-year rotating schedule. When your township enters its reassessment year, the Assessor recalculates your property's value. That cuts both ways.

The risk: your assessed value can rise, particularly where the market has appreciated.

The opportunity: you can appeal the new assessment with current comparable evidence.

Being outside your reassessment year does not bar an appeal. You can appeal your existing assessment in the intervening years too — the reassessment year is simply when the Assessor is actively re-deriving values.

## How to Appeal Your Rich Township Assessment

**Step 1: Find your assessment and your township**
Look up your PIN at the [Assessor's property search](https://www.cookcountyassessoril.gov/address-search). Your assessed value is also on your most recent tax bill. The Assessor mails notices in a reassessment year, but do not treat the arrival of mail as your clock — the published calendar is the authority, and mail can be delayed or misdirected.

**Step 2: Find comparable properties**
A first-level appeal shows that properties genuinely similar to yours — same class, similar size, similar age, nearby — are assessed lower, supported by relevant recent sales. This is what Rule 15 evidence refers to.

**Step 3: File before the last file date for Rich Township**
Confirm that date on the [Assessor's calendar](https://www.cookcountyassessoril.gov/assessment-calendar-and-deadlines) and file with the county, or use OverTaxed IL to prepare the packet you file yourself.
