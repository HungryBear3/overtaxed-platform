Municipality and township are not the same thing, and neighbouring parcels can fall differently than the village name suggests. Your township is decided by the county's record for your PIN — confirm it at the [Assessor's property search](https://www.cookcountyassessoril.gov/address-search).

## What the Reassessment Year Means

Cook County reassesses on a three-year rotating schedule, and 2026 is the south and west suburbs year. The Assessor re-derives every property's value using mass appraisal methods, which work from broad market data and do not account for an individual property's condition, recent renovations, or hyperlocal circumstances. Where comparable properties are assessed materially lower than yours, that gap is the argument.

You are not limited to the reassessment year. You can appeal your existing assessment in the intervening years as well; the reassessment year is simply when the Assessor is actively re-deriving values.

## The Appeal Process for Worth Township

**Step 1: Confirm your assessed value and your township** at the [Assessor's property search](https://www.cookcountyassessoril.gov/address-search). The Assessor mails reassessment notices, but do not treat the mail as your clock — the published calendar governs, and mail can be delayed or misdirected.

**Step 2: Confirm the last file date for Worth Township** on the [Assessor's calendar](https://www.cookcountyassessoril.gov/assessment-calendar-and-deadlines). Late filings are not accepted; there is no grace period and no postmark exception.

**Step 3: Assemble comparable evidence** — assessments of properties genuinely similar to yours, same class, similar size, similar age, nearby, supported by relevant recent sales. This is what Rule 15 evidence refers to.

**Step 4: File with the Assessor** before that date.

**Step 5: The Board of Review is a separate, later stage** with its own calendar and its own rules.
